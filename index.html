Problem statement

Reaching buyers and setting fair prices

Pattachitra artisans may face challenges reaching buyers directly and presenting their products to wider markets.

Limited market access and unclear price comparisons may affect their ability to negotiate fair prices.

Who is affected

Pattachitra artisans, their families, young craft workers and customers seeking authentic local artwork.

Why it matters now

A simple digital catalogue could help artisans display their work, set their own prices and receive buyer enquiries directly. Its benefits must be tested.

Local challenge

Understand artisans’ real selling difficulties before proposing a solution.

Evidence and scale

Raghurajpur is known for Pattachitra art. We will interview artisans and compare prices of similar artworks to understand local selling challenges.

Research and validation

Testing the problem before building

ProposedInterviews and surveys are planned. They stay labelled as proposed until fieldwork is complete.

Desk research

  • Research Raghurajpur’s Pattachitra community using reliable government sources, academic studies and dated news reports.
  • Review existing craft-selling channels and relevant support programmes.

User validation

  • Interview Pattachitra artisans about their current selling methods.
  • Ask how they record production costs and set selling prices.
  • Compare prices of similar artworks where reliable data is available.

Key findings to verify

Finding 1

Artisan dependence on intermediaries needs local verification.

Finding 2

Price transparency and direct market access need to be investigated.

Finding 3

The usefulness of digital selling must be tested with artisans.

How evidence will be shown

  • Verified government portals, academic studies and dated articles, each with an active link.
  • Every interview and survey labelled as proposed until fieldwork is complete.
  • Direct artisan quotes placed beside price comparisons of equivalent artwork.

Existing solutions

What artisans use today

Village and exhibition sales

What it does well

Buyers can inspect artwork and meet artisans directly.

Limitation or cost

Reach may depend on visitor numbers, events and travel costs.

Traders and intermediaries

What it does well

May connect artisans to wider markets and handle transport or bulk orders.

Limitation or cost

Price breakdowns and artisan earnings may not always be transparent.

Social media and direct orders

What it does well

Allows product promotion and direct communication with buyers.

Limitation or cost

Requires digital skills, product photos and order coordination.

Key insight

Existing channels help artisans reach buyers. Gaon To Growth aims to make product information, pricing and buyer enquiries easier to manage in one place.

Gaps and opportunity

What today’s channels still leave open

Access

A simple mobile-friendly catalogue and guided registration could help artisans with limited digital experience.

Affordability

Product prices and applicable charges stay visible. Platform fees and operating costs will be confirmed before the team promises low or zero fees.

Performance

Organised product listings and enquiry records could help artisans track customer interest and follow up on orders.

Local context

A simple interface and potential Odia-language support could improve accessibility. The team will test these features with local artisans.

Our opportunity

To create an accessible artisan-focused platform for product visibility, transparent pricing and direct buyer enquiries.

Proposed solution

One place for listings, prices and enquiries

Gaon To Growth is a proposed digital marketplace that helps Pattachitra artisans showcase handmade products, display prices and connect with potential buyers.

Simple listings. Transparent prices. Direct buyer enquiries.
  1. 01

    Input

    Artisans create a profile, list handmade products and set their own prices.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Each listing includes product photos, measurements, materials, price and artisan details.

  3. 03

    Process

    The platform displays product information and records customer enquiries and orders.

  4. 04

    Deliver

    Buyers contact artisans, while order, payment and delivery updates can be recorded where supported.

  5. 05

    Impact

    The project aims to improve product visibility, price transparency and buyer communication.

Tech stack and system design

Simple tools the team can realistically build with

Hardware

Standard mobile phones and simple photography setups will be used to capture artisan products.

Software

The website will use HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with Firebase or Supabase considered for storing product listings and order records.

Connectivity

The platform will use internet access where available, with local facilitators assisting artisans who need digital support.

Input

Artisan details, product images and prices.

Processing

Product catalogue management, price display and order tracking.

Output

Online product listings, customer enquiries and order updates.

Target audience and impact

Who it is for and how we will measure benefit

Primary users

Pattachitra artisans in Raghurajpur can use the platform to showcase their products and manage buyer enquiries.

Beneficiaries

The project aims to benefit artisan households, art lovers and future generations of craft workers through improved product visibility.

Early adopters

Begin with a small group of volunteer artisan families after confirming their interest and participation.

Possible measures of impact

  • Less time spent managing enquiries and orders.
  • Changes in selling expenses, where measurable.
  • Clearer records of prices and payments.
  • Number of active artisans and customers.
  • Changes in unsold stock, if reliably measured.
  • Number of artisans receiving orders.
  • Feedback from artisans and buyers.

Sustainability and scalability

Keeping it useful, affordable and maintainable

Financial

Initial funding may come from institutional support, innovation grants or CSR sponsorships. The pilot budget and running costs will be confirmed before the team commits.

Environmental

Reusing existing phones and using digital receipts where practical may reduce material use. Any reduction in packaging, travel or waste will be measured.

Social

Simple interfaces, artisan participation and control over product pricing will support local ownership.

Scalability and maintenance

After testing the pilot, trained local facilitators could help onboard more artisans and assess expansion to other craft clusters.

Next step

Develop and test a basic prototype.
